## Part 1 — Structured Summary

**Format:** exactly **seven elements**, in the order below, presented as a three-column table: **`#` / `Element` / `Sentence`**, with one row per element.

| # | Element | What the brief asks for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Topic** | The Software Engineering area or topic of the paper. |
| 2 | **Research question or problem** | A problem narrower than the topic, but one that exists beyond this particular paper. Do not introduce the paper's own technique, tool or dataset here. |
| 3 | **Prior work and gap** | The existing state of the art and its limitations **at the time the paper was published**, rather than the paper's own novelty or later research. |
| 4 | **Novelty / contribution** | A new technique or tool, hypothesis or theoretical account, or empirical angle. Keep methods and results separate from this element. |
| 5 | **Research method** | How the authors investigated, built or evaluated the contribution. Include study design and scale when relevant. |
| 6 | **Results** | Concrete outcomes or findings. Reflect numbers and comparisons reported in the paper, rather than only saying that the work is important. |
| 7 | **Claimed significance** | The significance or implications claimed by the authors in the paper. Do not simply repeat the results or describe actual impact ten years later or why the paper received an MIP award. |

### Length and writing rules

- The usual aim is **one sentence per element**, but the brief occasionally permits multiple sentences for an element. There is no exception-free requirement to write exactly seven sentences.
- The recommended length is **15–35 words per element**, or approximately **150–250 words** in total. These are guidelines; exceeding the recommended range does not itself lose marks.
- **Strict maximum: 100 words per element**, counting all sentences in that element. Exceeding it gives that element **0 marks**.
- A sentence must not cover multiple elements, such as mixing the gap with novelty or the method with results.
- Although each element has its own row, the Sentence column should read from top to bottom as **one coherent paragraph**. Connectives and pronouns may be used naturally across sentences.
- Audience: professional engineers, master's students or researchers in other SE areas who know the field but may not be familiar with the latest research on the paper's topic.
- Use your own words. Do not copy and paste sentences from the paper or other online sources, and do not rely only on the abstract.

## Part 2 — Personal Reflection

Write three **independent short answers**; they do not need to form a connected paragraph:

1. **Most interesting:** What did you find most interesting or surprising about this paper, and why?
2. **Least interesting / least convincing:** What did you find least interesting, or where do you think the paper is weakest or least convincing?
3. **Unclear:** What was unclear or hard to follow in the writing, method or results? If nothing was unclear, explain what made the paper easy to understand, such as a particular figure, running example or explanation of the method.

- The recommended length is **30–60 words per answer**. This is guidance.
- **Strict maximum: 100 words per answer**. An answer exceeding it receives **0 marks**.
- These must be your reactions to your own reading, tied to a particular claim, result, design choice or other specific part of the paper. They should not summarise the opinions of reviewers or later citing papers.
- A bare statement that nothing was unclear is too generic; give a specific explanation.

## Marking and easy-to-miss distinctions

The total is **100 marks**: each of the seven elements in Part 1 and each of the three answers in Part 2 is worth 10 marks. Each receives **0 / 5 / 10**.

The official page and brief highlight the following:

- Copying, serious inaccuracies or exceeding the 100-word limit can give an item 0 marks.
- **Problem:** Do not only state the paper's aim. The problem should matter beyond the paper's particular solution.
- **Prior work and gap:** Use prior work as it stood at the time of publication.
- **Method:** Include more than the type of study; describe the design and scale where relevant.
- **Results:** Report concrete findings. **Significance:** Preserve the authors' claim in the original paper. Keep these two elements separate.
- Reflections are assessed on specific engagement with the paper, rather than whether the marker agrees with your opinions.
